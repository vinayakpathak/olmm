# Delayed Market Making I: Slow Making, Fast Clearing

This note solves the version of delayed market making where the maker posts a quote now, but that quote becomes active only after a delay. Once the quote is hit, clearing in the reference market is immediate.

The key point is that this is still the same market-making problem as in `latex/colt.tex`, except that feedback is delayed.

## Model

Let

$$
\mathcal U = \{(B,A)\in[0,1]^2 : B\le A\}.
$$

At submission round $t$, the learner chooses $(B_t,A_t)\in\mathcal U$.

Assume there is an exogenous delay $D_t\in\{0,1,\dots,\tau\}$, so the quote submitted at round $t$ becomes executable at

$$
s_t = t + D_t.
$$

To keep the simple one-taker-per-round protocol well defined, we only need that executions are uniquely matched to future rounds. So assume that the map $t \mapsto s_t$ is injective:

$$
\text{if } t \neq t', \text{ then } s_t \neq s_{t'}.
$$

This means each future round executes at most one previously submitted quote. A later quote may still execute before an earlier one.

At round $s_t$:

- the arriving taker has valuation $Z_{s_t}$,
- the clearing-market price is $P_{s_t}$,
- the reward generated by the action chosen at round $t$ is

$$
u_t^{\mathrm{make}}(B,A)
=
(P_{s_t}-B)\mathbf 1\{B\ge Z_{s_t}\}
+
(A-P_{s_t})\mathbf 1\{A<Z_{s_t}\}.
$$

The learner observes the full feedback for round $t$ only at time $s_t$.

The regret against the best fixed bid-ask pair is

$$
R_T^{\mathrm{make}}
=
\sup_{(B,A)\in\mathcal U}
\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{t=1}^T u_t^{\mathrm{make}}(B,A)\right]
-
\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{t=1}^T u_t^{\mathrm{make}}(B_t,A_t)\right].
$$

## Reduction To The Base Problem

Define the transformed sequence

$$
\widetilde P_t := P_{s_t},
\qquad
\widetilde Z_t := Z_{s_t}.
$$

Then

$$
u_t^{\mathrm{make}}(B,A)
=
(\widetilde P_t-B)\mathbf 1\{B\ge \widetilde Z_t\}
+
(A-\widetilde P_t)\mathbf 1\{A<\widetilde Z_t\},
$$

which is exactly the one-round utility from `latex/colt.tex`, evaluated on the transformed pair $(\widetilde P_t,\widetilde Z_t)$.

So the only change is that the feedback for round $t$ arrives $D_t$ rounds late.

The structural assumptions from `latex/colt.tex` survive this transformation:

- If the original process satisfies `(adv+lip)`, then the transformed sequence still has an $L$-Lipschitz valuation CDF at every round.
- If the original process satisfies `(iid+iv)` and the delays are exogenous, then the transformed sequence is again `(iid+iv)`.

## Delay-Robust Version Of `M3`

Run $\tau+1$ independent copies of `M3`, call them

$$
\mathcal M_0,\mathcal M_1,\dots,\mathcal M_\tau.
$$

At real round $t$, use copy

$$
j = t \bmod (\tau+1).
$$

When the feedback of round $t$ arrives at time $s_t$, feed that feedback to the same copy $\mathcal M_j$ that produced $(B_t,A_t)$.

This works because the same copy is reused only every $\tau+1$ rounds, while every feedback arrives within at most $\tau$ rounds. Therefore each copy sees immediate feedback on its own subsequence. No monotonicity of the execution times is needed for this argument.

## Elementary Theorem

Assume the base algorithm `M3` from `latex/colt.tex` satisfies

$$
R_n \le c\, n^{2/3}
$$

for every horizon $n$ under either `(adv+lip)` or `(iid+iv)`.

Then the delayed wrapper above satisfies

$$
R_T^{\mathrm{make}}
\le
c\,(\tau+1)^{1/3}T^{2/3}.
$$

Using the constants from `latex/colt.tex`, we get

$$
R_T^{\mathrm{make}}
\le
(2L+100)(\tau+1)^{1/3}T^{2/3}
\qquad\text{under `(adv+lip)`},
$$

and

$$
R_T^{\mathrm{make}}
\le
102(\tau+1)^{1/3}T^{2/3}
\qquad\text{under `(iid+iv)`}.
$$

## Proof

Let $n_j$ be the number of real rounds assigned to copy $\mathcal M_j$.

Each copy sees an ordinary immediate-feedback market-making instance of length $n_j$, so its regret is at most

$$
c\,n_j^{2/3}.
$$

Summing over all copies,

$$
R_T^{\mathrm{make}}
\le
c\sum_{j=0}^{\tau} n_j^{2/3}.
$$

Now apply Hölder's inequality in the form

$$
\sum_{j=0}^{\tau} n_j^{2/3}
\le
(\tau+1)^{1/3}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\tau} n_j\right)^{2/3}.
$$

Since $\sum_{j=0}^{\tau} n_j = T$, this yields

$$
R_T^{\mathrm{make}}
\le
c\,(\tau+1)^{1/3}T^{2/3}.
$$

## Interpretation

For constant delay, the regret rate stays of order $T^{2/3}$.

So the slow-making version is not a new learning problem. It is the same `M3` problem with delayed feedback.

However, the factor $(\tau+1)^{1/3}$ should be viewed only as the price of the crude interleaving wrapper above, not as the likely optimal dependence on delay.

## Sharper Delay Dependence

The proof above freezes one copy of `M3` for $\tau+1$ rounds on each subsequence. That is simple, but wasteful.

A better approach is to keep the discretization from `latex/colt.tex` and replace the immediate-feedback $K$-armed bandit routine inside each discretized subproblem by a delayed-feedback adversarial bandit algorithm.

Let

$$
D := \sum_{t=1}^T D_t
$$

be the cumulative delay, and let

$$
\widetilde L :=
\begin{cases}
L & \text{under `(adv+lip)`},\\
1 & \text{under `(iid+iv)`},
\end{cases}
$$

exactly as in `latex/colt.tex`.

Off-the-shelf delayed adversarial bandit results give regret bounds of the form

$$
\widetilde O\!\left(\sqrt{KT + D}\right)
$$

up to logarithmic factors in $K$. Concretely, Bistritz et al. 2019 prove a bound of order

$$
O\!\left(\sqrt{\log K \, (KT + D)}\right).
$$

Plugging such a delayed bandit subroutine into the same discretization argument as in `latex/colt.tex` yields

$$
R_T^{\mathrm{make}}
\lesssim
\widetilde L\,\frac{T}{K}
+
\sqrt{\log K \, (KT + D)},
$$

up to constants and logarithmic factors. The first term is exactly the same discretization error as in `latex/colt.tex`.

If the delays are uniformly bounded by $D_t \le \tau$, then $D \le \tau T$, and therefore

$$
R_T^{\mathrm{make}}
\lesssim
\widetilde L\,\frac{T}{K}
+
\sqrt{KT\log K}
+
\sqrt{\tau T \log K}.
$$

The important point is that the delay term is independent of $K$. So $K$ must still be chosen by balancing the original discretization term $\widetilde L T/K$ against the $K$-dependent bandit term $\sqrt{KT\log K}$.

Thus the natural choice is

$$
K \asymp (\widetilde L^2 T/\log T)^{1/3},
$$

up to logarithmic self-consistency. With this choice,

$$
R_T^{\mathrm{make}}
\lesssim
\widetilde L^{1/3} T^{2/3}(\log T)^{1/3}
+
\sqrt{\tau T \log T}.
$$

So, up to logarithmic factors, the delay contribution should be of order $\sqrt{\tau T}$ rather than $\tau^{1/3}T^{2/3}$, while the original discretization/bandit tradeoff from `latex/colt.tex` remains intact.

## What This Suggests About Lower Bounds

The no-delay lower bound from `latex/colt.tex` already gives

$$
\Omega(T^{2/3}).
$$

For the delay-dependent part, delayed adversarial bandit lower bounds point to a $\sqrt{D}$-type obstruction, which under bounded delays becomes $\sqrt{\tau T}$ up to logs.

This suggests the natural conjecture

$$
\Omega\!\bigl(\max\{T^{2/3}, \sqrt{\tau T}\}\bigr)
$$

up to logarithmic factors, and possibly even the additive form

$$
\Omega\!\bigl(T^{2/3} + \sqrt{\tau T}\bigr).
$$

This note does not prove such a lower bound for delayed market making. The point here is only that the elementary $(\tau+1)^{1/3}T^{2/3}$ bound is almost certainly not optimal in its dependence on $\tau$.

## Remark On Random Delays

The interleaving proof above only needs the deterministic upper bound $D_t\le \tau$.

If the delays are random and unbounded, one should use a standard delayed-bandit wrapper instead of the simple $\tau+1$-copy construction. In that case one expects a bound of the form

$$
\widetilde O\!\left(T^{2/3} + \sqrt{\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^T D_t}\right),
$$

after discretization, up to additional logarithmic factors. This extension is not proved in this note; it is an inference from the general delayed-feedback literature, not a theorem established here.

## References

- Pooria Joulani, Andras Gyorgy, Csaba Szepesvari, [*Online Learning under Delayed Feedback*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/joulani13.html), ICML 2013.
  This is the foundational black-box delayed-feedback reduction paper. The $\tau+1$-copy wrapper used in this note is an elementary bounded-delay special case of that general perspective.

- Ilai Bistritz, Zhengyuan Zhou, Xi Chen, Nicholas Bambos, Jose Blanchet, [*Online EXP3 Learning in Adversarial Bandits with Delayed Feedback*](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/ae2a2db40a12ec0131d48acc1218d2ef-Abstract.html), NeurIPS 2019.
  This gives a concrete adversarial delayed-bandit regret bound of order $O(\sqrt{\log K (KT + D)})$, which is the basis for the sharper $\sqrt{\tau T}$-type discussion above.

- Saeed Masoudian, Julian Zimmert, Yevgeny Seldin, [*A Best-of-Both-Worlds Algorithm for Bandits with Delayed Feedback*](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Dqcoao24G8s), NeurIPS 2022.
  For fixed delay $d$, this paper proves adversarial regret of order $O(\sqrt{TK} + \sqrt{dT\log K})$ and also gives a lower bound matching the delay-dependent part achieved by the skipping-based approach.

- Andras Gyorgy, Pooria Joulani, [*Adapting to Delays and Data in Adversarial Multi-Armed Bandits*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/gyorgy21a.html), ICML 2021.
  This is a useful modern reference for variable delays and cumulative-delay bounds in adversarial bandits. The final remark about handling unbounded/random delays should be read in the spirit of this line of work.
